DESCRIPTION
200,000 years ago, falling asleep was the most dangerous thing a human could do. Lions, hyenas and leopards hunted at night — and you had to be unconscious for seven hours.

So how did our ancestors survive the night? In this video: why your mattress is dirtier than a chimp's nest, the leopard-bitten skull from Swartkrans, the 200,000-year-old beds with built-in bug spray, the Hadza "night watch" (everyone was asleep at once for just 18 minutes in 20 nights), why half your brain stays awake in hotels, the forgotten "first sleep" and "second sleep", sleep paralysis and the night hag, stone pillows — and the wild idea that sleeping on the ground helped build the human mind.
